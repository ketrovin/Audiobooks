CHAPTER 20: NORFOLK ANCHORAGE

By the following evening, the naval task force had reached the entrance to the Chesapeake Bay, slipping past the concrete caissons of the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel beneath a low, rain-slicked sky the color of a bruised plum.

Naval Station Norfolk, the largest naval base on the planet, was a graveyard of smoldering steel.

From the bridge of the *San Antonio*, Arthur Pendelton stared in stunned silence at the waterfront piers. Pier 12, where the nuclear aircraft carriers normally berthed, was a charred ruin of twisted gantry cranes and blackened concrete. The supercarrier USS *Harry S. Truman* sat listing twenty degrees to port, her flight deck scorched by an internal ordnance fire, thick black smoke still curling from her aircraft elevators.

Along the shoreline of Willoughby Spit, hundreds of military transport trucks, Humvees, and civilian ambulances had been pushed into the bay to form makeshift sea barricades. On the concrete quays, thousands of infected sailors, dockworkers, and civilian dependents roamed in massive, shifting packs, their movements visible through the rain like a dark, living carpet spreading across the concrete.

"The Atlantic Fleet headquarters fell yesterday morning," Captain Thomas Vance said, his face illuminated by the green glow of the tactical plotting table. "The infection broke through the perimeter security gates on Hampton Boulevard when forty thousand refugees from Virginia Beach stormed the base seeking naval evacuation. The base commander ordered the ammunition depots at Craney Island flooded to prevent catastrophic cook-offs, but the fuel storage farm at Pier 14 is still pressurized."

Marcus walked over to the chart table, leaning his heavy hands against the brass rim. "Pier 14? That's the high-capacity marine bunkering pier. If those pipelines are still intact, their auxiliary pumps are gravity-fed from the above-ground storage tanks on the hill."

"They are," Thomas said. "Our scouts report the storage farm has three million gallons of marine diesel. But the pier is completely surrounded. The access causeway is choked with stalled fuel trucks, and there are at least two thousand hosts patrolling the concrete aprons."

"We don't need the causeway," Jonah Drake said, stepping up to the table. "We insert a tactical engineering team directly from the water onto the pier head using the ship's amphibious landing craft. We drop the suction hoses, couple directly to the naval bunkering manifold, and pump two hundred thousand gallons into the *San Antonio*'s reserve tanks."

"And if the manifold is dry?" Arthur asked.

"Then we don't make Key West," Drake answered flatly. "The *San Antonio* burns thirty gallons of diesel per nautical mile at convoy speed. Our fuel reserves are at eighteen percent. If we don't bunker at Pier 14, this task force goes dead in the water off the Outer Banks."

Marcus reached out, his thick fingers tapping the steel jaw of his track wrench. "I know the mechanical bunkering valves on Pier 14. They're ten-inch Crane flanged gate valves with bronze handwheels. If the automated actuators are dead, they can only be cracked by hand using a thirty-six-inch lever."

He looked at his cousin Thomas, then at Drake.

"Get your landing craft ready, Captain," Marcus said. "I'm going ashore."
