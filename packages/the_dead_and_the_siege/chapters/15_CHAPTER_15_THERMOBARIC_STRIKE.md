CHAPTER 15: THERMOBARIC STRIKE

The lead infected was a woman in an evening dress, her face torn to the bone, her hair whipped by the high-altitude wind. She leaped across the steel grating with terrifying speed, launching herself through the air directly at Marcus.

Marcus didn't try to swing the wrenchhe didn't have the room. He used the thirty-six-inch carbon-steel bar as a two-handed horizontal block, catching the woman across the throat mid-air.

The momentum knocked him backward against the exterior railing, eight hundred feet above the street. The woman's jaws snapped shut inches from Marcus's nose, black, foul-smelling saliva spraying across his face.

Marcus hooked his left leg around the base of the railing, torqued his upper body with all the leverage in his massive frame, and hurled the woman over the four-foot parapet.

She fell into the freezing void, tumbling end over end toward the dark streets below, swallowed instantly by the fog.

"Marcus! Grab the ladder!" Drake roared from the hovering helicopter.

Marcus turned and leaped. His hands locked onto the heavy nylon rungs of the ladder just as the Bell 412 surged upward into the gale.

Behind him on the platform, five infected threw themselves off the edge in a blind attempt to reach the climbing skids. They missed, their hands clutching empty air as they plunged eight hundred feet into the darkness.

As Marcus pulled himself up the rungs into the warm cabin, Drake reached down, grabbed the collar of his jacket, and hauled him across the door sill.

Marcus collapsed onto the cabin floor, breathing hard, his muscles trembling with total exhaustion. Brenda knelt beside him, wiping the black bile from his forehead with a clean towel.

"We're clear!" Drake shouted to the cockpit. "Get us out of the midtown corridor! Execute Strike Protocol Alpha!"

Tommy Vance banked the helicopter hard to the south, climbing through the clouds toward the safety of Upper New York Bay.

Through the cabin windows, Arthur Pendelton, Marcus Vance, and Dr. Sarah Mercer watched the midtown skyline.

High above the clouds, a flight of two Air Force F-15E Strike Eagles shrieked through the night air at supersonic speed. From their underwing pylons dropped two heavy BLU-118 thermobaric ordnance canisters, their guidance fins guiding them directly toward the intersection of 42nd Street and Park Avenue.

The detonation did not produce a conventional fireball.

It produced an incandescent, oxygen-consuming wave of extreme pressure and three-thousand-degree heat that rolled down the street canyons like a tidal wave of liquid sun. In an instant, every window within ten blocks shattered into powdered silica. The air was sucked from the street canyons in a massive, inward-collapsing vacuum, and the entire surface swarm across forty square blocks was consumed by an incinerating firestorm that turned bone and tissue into grey ash in less than a second.

The shockwave hit the retreating Bell 412 two miles away, lifting the tail boom and rattling every rivet in the airframe before smoothing out.

Looking back through the rear door, Marcus watched the center of New York City burn beneath a rising mushroom cloud of white-hot ash.
