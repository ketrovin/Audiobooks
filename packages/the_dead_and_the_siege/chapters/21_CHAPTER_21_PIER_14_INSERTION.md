CHAPTER 21: PIER 14 INSERTION

The Landing Craft Utility (LCU 1643) dropped out of the *San Antonio*'s flooded well-deck into the oily, debris-choked waters of Hampton Roads.

LCU 1643 was a heavy steel flat-bottomed barge, one hundred and thirty-five feet long, powered by twin diesel engines that growled with a deep, subterranean rumble. Its open cargo deck carried sixty combat-equipped Marines, Captain Jonah Drake's tactical team, Marcus Vance, Arthur Pendelton, and two massive four-inch reinforced high-pressure fuel transfer hoses coiled like giant sea snakes.

The night was pitch black. Freezing rain lashed the steel ramp as the landing craft rounded the tip of Sewell's Point, heading toward the concrete fingers of Pier 14.

Ahead of them, the pier jutted sixteen hundred feet into the dark watera concrete monolith lined with steel bollards, heavy industrial piping, and massive fuel bunkering towers. The only light came from the dull orange glow of a burning fuel barge stranded two miles up the Elizabeth River.

"Two hundred yards," the LCU coxswain called out through the wheelhouse speaker. "Drop ramp on my mark!"

Marcus tightened his grip on his twelve-pound track wrench. Beside him, Arthur Pendelton checked the optical sight on his issued carbine, his face set in hard, unforgiving lines.

"Arthur," Marcus said quietly over the engine noise. "You don't have to be on this ramp. You're an airport supervisor. This is an amphibious assault."

Arthur spat over the steel bulwark into the dark water. "Thirty years in Queens, Marcus. I've dealt with blizzards that buried twelve hundred planes, transit strikes that brought two million angry commuters into my concourses, and airline executives who thought they owned the earth. A few thousand rabid corpses on a concrete pier aren't running me off. Besides, somebody's got to keep an eye on your six while you turn those valves."

The steel bow ramp slammed down onto the concrete apron of Pier 14 with a deafening metallic crash that sent sparks flying into the rain.

"Go! Go! Go!" Drake yelled, charging down the ramp into the darkness.

The Marines fanned out in a wide tactical horseshoe across the pier, their weapon lights cutting through the driving rain.

Immediately, the darkness responded.

From behind the shipping containers, the fuel testing sheds, and the maintenance shacks along the pier, the infected surged forward. They were former sailors in blue utility uniforms, civilian dockworkers in greasy yellow overalls, and security guards whose gear was stained with black blood. They came in an unbroken, sprinting pack, their boots thundering against the wet concrete, their jaws snapping with that terrifying, rhythmic clatter.

"Open fire!" Drake roared.

Fifty rifles opened up simultaneously. In the enclosed space of the pier, the muzzle blasts were a continuous, blinding strobelight of white fire. The heavy 5.56mm rounds tore through the charging horde, shattering limbs and disintegrating skulls, dropping the lead hosts in tangled heaps on the concrete.

But they didn't slow down. The ones behind leaped over the fallen, their milky eyes reflecting the gunfire, their bloody fingers reaching for the Marines' throats.

"Marcus! Manifold station four!" Drake shouted, firing short, controlled bursts into the crowd. "Get the lines connected!"
