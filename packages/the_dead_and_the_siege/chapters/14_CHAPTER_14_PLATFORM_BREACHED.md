CHAPTER 14: PLATFORM BREACHED

Marcus looked down between his boots.

In the beam of his headlamp, twenty rungs below, the first host was climbing. It was a man in the shredded remains of a security uniform, his face covered in dried black blood, his teeth bare in an unyielding snarl. He was moving up the ladder two rungs at a time, his movements jerky, robotic, and shockingly fast.

"Climb, Pete!" Marcus roared, unhooking his twelve-pound track wrench from his shoulder strap with his left hand while clinging to the ladder with his right arm hooked through a rung.

Marcus waited until the creature's fingers touched the rung immediately beneath his boots.

With a short, brutal downward jab, Marcus drove the heavy steel wrench into the creature's face. The solid impact crushed the nasal bridge and the frontal bone with a sickening wet crunch. The creature let out a choked hiss, its hands losing their grip, and it fell backward into the dark void of the shaft.

It plummeted five hundred feet in total silence before striking the metal roof of the elevator cab below with a distant, hollow *THUD*.

But behind it came three more.

Marcus didn't wait. He scrambled up the remaining twenty rungs to the 60th-floor maintenance landing, where Brenda and Pete were hauling themselves through the access door onto the exterior staging deck.

Marcus rolled through the doorway onto the steel grating, spun around, and slammed the heavy iron door shut. He grabbed a six-foot piece of structural angle iron that lay on the deck and jammed it through the heavy door handles, wedging it against the concrete doorframe.

The door shuddered immediately as bodies began slamming against the other side, the metal booming like a bass drum.

"Out on the deck!" Marcus yelled, turning toward the open air.

The 60th-floor mechanical deck was an open-air staging platform that wrapped around the exterior of the tower, eight hundred feet above Manhattan. The wind up here was ferociousa howling, fifty-knot gale that whipped sleet and rain across the steel-grate flooring.

Hovering twenty feet off the deck, its skids level with the railing, was the Bell 412.

Pilot Tommy Vance had the helicopter locked in a masterclass hover, fighting the severe crosswinds that curled around the building's corners. Drake stood in the open doorway, holding a heavy nylon extraction ladder that trailed across the steel grating of the platform.

"Hook in!" Drake screamed through his bullhorn. "One at a time! Hook to the ladder!"

Pete went first. He lunged across the grating, grabbed the heavy nylon rungs, and snapped his carabiner into the center line. Drake hauled the boy up into the cabin like a sack of mail.

Brenda went next, moving with calm, disciplined precision, clipping her harness in and climbing smoothly into the cabin.

Just as Marcus reached for the ladder, the steel angle iron barring the service door snapped in half under the collective weight of dozens of bodies.

The heavy door was ripped off its hinges, and a wave of infected spilled onto the 60th-floor deck.
