CHAPTER 7: ROOFTOP INSERTION

The ascent in the manual service lift was an agonizing crawl through a vertical concrete chimney filled with the stench of burning hospital waste and ozone. The lift was an open iron cage, its cables groaning under the weight of Drake, Arthur, Sarah Mercer, and Bobby Harris.

Outside the iron mesh of the cage, as they passed the eighth floor, Arthur looked through the wire window into the main corridor. The entire floor was ablaze. Hospital beds had been piled into makeshift barricades that were now burning with fierce, chemical intensity, casting long, frantic shadows across walls painted with handprints of black blood. Through the smoke, dozens of infected were roaming the halls, tearing through linen closets and pharmacy cabinets, their jaws snapping in the empty air.

"They're searching every room," Arthur said, his voice grim as he reloaded his Glock magazine with cold, steady fingers. "They aren't just reacting to sound anymore. They're systematically clearing the structure from the ground up."

"They follow thermal gradients," Sarah Mercer said, adjusting the straps of the cryo-pack on her shoulders. "Their body temperature drops to forty degrees Fahrenheit within an hour of death, but their ocular nerves develop extreme sensitivity to infrared wavelengths. They can see human body heat through drywall and hollow-core wooden doors. That's why hiding in closets doesn't work."

The lift clunked to a halt at the roof staging vestibule. Drake kicked open the accordion gate, and they spilled out onto the wind-swept concrete expanse of Bellevue Hospital's northern roof deck.

The rain had turned to freezing sleet, pelting their faces with needle-sharp shards of ice. The two Bell 412 helicopters were still idling on the non-skid pads, their rotor blades cutting through the mist in shimmering discs of moisture. Kowalski stood by the open door of the lead aircraft, firing short, controlled bursts from his carbine down the exterior fire escape stairs that ran along the southern facade of the building.

"Drake!" Kowalski bellowed through his headset. "They're coming up the outside! They're climbing the iron fire escapes! There are hundreds of them on the brickwork!"

Arthur ran to the southern parapet and looked down.

The sight made his stomach lurch. The brick facade of the fourteen-story hospital was covered in climbing figures. They were scaling the rusted iron fire escapes three abreast, scrambling over the railings, using their bare hands and feet to wedge themselves into masonry joints, climbing with the speed and agility of hunting spiders. Those who slipped and fell into the courtyard twelve floors below didn't even make a soundthey simply struck the pavement with a wet thud, and the ones behind them climbed over their crushed bodies without breaking stride.

"Kincaid!" a voice shouted from the roof access door on the western stair tower.

Arthur spun around, raising his carbine.

Two figures in dark navy FDNY paramedic uniforms burst through the metal door, slamming it shut behind them and dropping a heavy wooden 4x4 beam across the iron brackets. It was Thomas Kincaid and Elena Cruz, their uniforms torn, their faces streaked with blood and soot.

"Don't shoot!" Kincaid yelled, holding his hands high, the heavy halligan bar dangling from his wrist lanyard. "We're paramedics! FDNY EMS 12! We came up through the heating plant tunnels!"

Drake stepped between them, his shotgun leveled at Kincaid's chest. "Are either of you bitten? Check your sleeves! Now!"

Kincaid immediately stripped off his heavy turnout coat, turning in a full circle with his arms raised. "Clean! Both of us! We took three scratches from broken glass in the basement, but no bites! Check her!"

Elena stripped off her jacket, her breath coming in ragged gasps, exposing her forearms. Her skin was pale and shivering in the sleet, but unblemished by teeth marks.

Arthur lowered his weapon. "They're clean, Drake. They're FDNY. We take them."

"Bird Two is full!" Kowalski shouted, waving from the second helicopter where Miller and the data storage cases were already secured. "Drake, we are taking fire from street level! Some of the armed looters on First Avenue are shooting at the rotor discs!"

A stray rifle round snapped through the sleet, punching a clean, jagged hole through the thin aluminum tail boom of Kowalski's aircraft with a sharp *PING*.

"Mount up!" Drake roared, shoving Sarah Mercer, Kincaid, and Elena Cruz toward the open cabin of the lead Bell 412. "Arthur, Bobby, get in! Kowalski, lift Bird Two now! Clear the deck!"
