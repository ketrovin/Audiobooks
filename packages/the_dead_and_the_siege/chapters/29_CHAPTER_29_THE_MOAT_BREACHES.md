CHAPTER 29: THE MOAT BREACHES

The first wave of debris hit the outer counterscarp wall at midnight.

A forty-foot fiberglass motor yacht, torn from its moorings in Key West and swept sixty miles across the Gulf, struck the granite seawall at twenty knots, disintegrating in a massive explosion of white fiberglass splinters, gasoline vapors, and shattered foam.

Behind it came the armada of the fallen.

They were not swimming. Human beings cannot swim in a twenty-foot sea whipped by hurricane-force winds. But the infected did not need to breathe, and they did not tire. Hundreds of them were lashed to overturned life rafts, clinging to wooden deck timbers, or floating high in the foam buoyed by orange commercial life vests that kept their heads above water.

And thousands more were walking along the submerged sand spit, their bodies submerged three feet beneath the rushing storm surge, their feet finding purchase on the crushed coral bottom as the ferocious eight-knot tide carried them forward like an unstoppable underwater landslide.

Arthur Pendelton stood in the covered gallery of Tier Two, looking down through the brick embrasure.

The moat, normally a placid turquoise channel, had become a churning cauldron of black water and white froth. The storm surge had overwhelmed the outer granite counterscarp, flooding the moat until the water level reached the top of the outer retaining wall.

And in the water, packing the eighty-foot channel from wall to wall, were hundreds of bodies.

They were being swept into the moat through the breached western sluice gate in an unbroken, churning torrent. As their bodies slammed against the eight-foot-thick brick walls of the fort, their fingers hooked into the masonry joints, seeking purchase. Those behind climbed over the shoulders of the submerged hosts, creating a writhing, living pyramid of bone and teeth that began climbing toward the second-tier gun embrasures twenty feet above the water.

"They're scaling the brickwork!" Arthur yelled into his radio. "Tier One casemates are submerged! They're coming up the outer wall!"

Inside the sally port tunnel, the iron portcullis shuddered with a deep, sickening boom.

Marcus Vance stood behind the steel mesh gate, his twelve-pound track wrench held in both hands. Through the iron slats, he could see the water rising. The storm surge had flooded the outer entrance, and now, pressed against the outside of the portcullis, were dozens of pale, waterlogged faces.

Their eyes were milky white, stripped of all human intelligence, their jaws snapping tirelessly against the cold steel bars. With every surging wave, the mass of bodies was forced tighter against the grating, their collective weight exerting thousands of pounds of hydraulic pressure against the mounting bolts.

"Brenda!" Marcus shouted over the roar of the wind. "The lower anchors are pulling free from the masonry! The lime mortar is giving way under the surge!"

"I've got the diesel generator tied to the portcullis frame!" Brenda yelled back from the entrance to the transformer vault. "I can energize the entire iron gate with six hundred volts of three-phase current from the ship's auxiliary feed!"

"Clear the tunnel!" Marcus roared, leaping back onto the dry raised concrete threshold of the Stage Three gate. "Energize the steel!"

Brenda slammed the massive copper knife switch closed.

A blinding blue arc of electrical flame exploded across the iron portcullis with a deafening *SNAP* like a lightning bolt striking a transformer. Six hundred volts of high-amperage current surged through the iron bars and into the salt water.

The effect was instantaneous and horrific.

The water inside the sally port tunnel erupted into boiling white steam. The bodies pressed against the grating convulsed violently, their nervous systems instantly incinerated by the massive electrical discharge. Thick, black smoke rolled from their waterlogged clothing as flesh and muscle charred to ash in seconds, breaking their grip on the steel.

Dozens of charred, smoking corpses slumped backward into the churning water, swept away by the receding surge into the moat.

"It works!" Brenda cried out, her face illuminated by the blue electrical arcing.

"It works for as long as the generator has fuel!" Marcus warned, resetting his grip on his iron wrench. "Drake! How are the upper parapets holding?!"
