CHAPTER 8: BELLEVUE ROOFTOP EVACUATION

Bird Two lifted first.

Its turbine engines screamed as the pilot pulled maximum pitch on the collective, the skids leaving the non-skid roof deck in a swirl of sleet and exhaust. The second helicopter rose twenty feet into the night air, banking hard east over the dark, turbulent waters of the East River, heading toward the safety of the open harbor.

Just as the lead helicopter prepared to follow, the heavy wooden beam barring the western stair tower door snapped in half with the sound of a dry branch cracking.

The metal door burst open, and a wave of infected poured onto the gravel roof deck.

At the front of the pack was a massive man in a torn security guard uniform, his face half-crushed, his hands clutching a rusted steel conduit pipe. He charged across the twenty yards of open gravel toward the idling Bell 412, moving with terrifying speed, his boots churning up the roofing ballast.

"Go! Go! Go!" Drake screamed, leaning out of the open side door, firing his shotgun into the charging crowd.

The blast took the lead guard in the chest, the heavy buckshot lifting him off his feet and slamming him into the gravel, but two more leaped over his thrashing body without pausing. One of them, a woman in hospital scrubs whose fingers had worn down to raw bone, lunged at the right landing skid just as the helicopter began to rise.

Her bloody hands clamped onto the tubular aluminum skid with superhuman force.

The helicopter listed violently to the right, the rotor blades dipping dangerously close to the concrete parapet wall. Inside the cockpit, Tommy Vance fought the cyclic with both hands, shouting over the turbine roar. "I've got dynamic rollover! She's dragging the starboard skid down! Clear that gear, Drake, or we're clipping the wall!"

Arthur Pendelton didn't wait for Drake to reload.

He lunged forward to the door sill, hooked his left arm around the cabin interior cargo strap, and swung his heavy six-cell steel flashlight down with all the leverage in his broad shoulders.

The solid aluminum head of the flashlight struck the woman's wrists once, twice, crushing the radial bones with a sickening crunch. Her grip broke, and she fell backward, tumbling sixty feet down into the dark courtyard below.

Free of the drag, the Bell 412 surged upward into the storm, clearing the parapet wall by less than four feet.

Arthur pulled himself back into the cabin, breathing in ragged gasps, his knuckles bloody, the rain streaming down his weathered face. He reached out and hauled the heavy sliding cabin door shut, latching it tight against the freezing Atlantic gale.

Inside the cabin, the only light came from the dim green tactical instrument panel in the cockpit.

Sarah Mercer sat slumped against the forward bulkhead, her arms wrapped tightly around the nylon pack containing the frozen pathogen samples. Elena Cruz was kneeling beside Kincaid, bandaging a deep cut on his forehead with a sterile gauze pad from her medic pouch.

Arthur collapsed onto the canvas bench seat next to Bobby Harris, resting his head back against the vibrating aluminum frame of the fuselage. He closed his eyes, his hands trembling with an exhaustion that went deeper than bone.

"Arthur," Bobby whispered, staring out the small oval window into the pitch-black void below. "Where are we going now? JFK is gone. The hospital is gone. Where is there left to land?"

Captain Jonah Drake sat opposite them, field-stripping his shotgun to clear the soot from the chamber, his face carved from grey stone.

"We aren't landing in New York," Drake said without looking up. "We're meeting the staging convoy at the Navy Yard in Brooklyn, and then we take the sub-level transit line down into the Grand Central transformer vaults to pull Marcus Vance's engineering crew. After that, we hit the water."

Arthur opened his eyes, looking through the cockpit windshield.

Beneath the helicopter's nose, the dark ribbon of the East River wound through a silent, dead metropolis. Not a single light burned across the five boroughs. The bridgesthe Williamsburg, the Manhattan, the Brooklynwere dark steel skeletons choked with motionless, frozen traffic.

Part 1 was over. The siege of the city was lost. And the long, dark retreat into the subterranean depths had just begun.
