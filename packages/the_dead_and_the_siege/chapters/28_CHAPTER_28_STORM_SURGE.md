CHAPTER 28: STORM SURGE

The storm hit Fort Jefferson with the savage fury of a physical hammer.

By six in the evening, the sun was completely blotted out by a roaring, pitch-black vortex of clouds that seemed to scrape the top of the fort's brick parapets. The wind shrieked through the empty gun embrasures like a thousand jet engines, whipping the salt spray into a blinding, horizontal white sheet that stung exposed skin like birdshot.

In the deep-water channel, the *San Antonio* and the *Kearsarge* had already vanished, their grey silhouettes swallowed by the mountainous twenty-foot grey combers as they steamed south into the open Gulf.

On Garden Key, humanity withdrew behind sixteen million bricks.

Marcus Vance, Brenda, and Arthur Pendelton stood inside the vaulted brick tunnel of the sally port, hauling on the heavy iron turnbuckles that secured the outer storm barricade. The wooden wharf outside was already completely submerged beneath six feet of churning, white-capped sea. Waves were crashing over the outer counterscarp wall, pouring into the eighty-foot moat in a thundering, foaming waterfall that raised the water level to within two feet of the drawbridge sill.

"Torque those bolts down!" Marcus bellowed over the roar of the tempest. "Brenda, drop the second iron grate!"

Brenda pulled the release lever on the overhead block-and-tackle. The heavy, four-inch structural steel portcullis dropped with a deafening metallic clang, its sharpened iron tines burying themselves four inches deep into the concrete threshold. Behind it, they lowered the massive four-inch-thick white oak inner drawbridge, securing it with four heavy steel dogging bars.

"Sally port is sealed!" Brenda shouted, wiping salt water and rain from her eyes.

"What about the lower gun embrasures?" Arthur asked, his carbine slung across his chest.

"Every casemate on Tier One has its heavy oak storm shutters bolted from the inside," Marcus said. "Three-inch white pine backed by steel plate. Unless an eighty-ton boulder hits the wall, nothing comes through those ports except salt spray."

Suddenly, a loud, explosive crack echoed from the top tier of the fortress.

Arthur sprinted up the granite spiral staircase of Bastion 1, taking the steps three at a time, emerging onto the wind-lashed terreplein.

The main communications mast, a sixty-foot steel lattice tower mounted on the southern parapet, had sheared its western guy wire under a ninety-knot gust. The heavy steel tower buckled with a screech of tearing metal, collapsing across the parapet and plunging into the foaming moat sixty feet below.

With it went all short-wave radio antennas, the marine VHF transceivers, and the satellite uplink dish.

Arthur stood on the parapet, his fingers clamped around the brick rim, staring out into the roaring black void to the southwest.

Through the sheets of driving rain, illuminated by continuous flashes of green sheet lightning, he saw it.

Two miles away, the massive white hull of the *Ocean Empress* had broken in half across Loggerhead Reef. The three-hundred-foot bow section had rolled onto its side in the surf, but the seven-hundred-foot stern superstructure had lifted off the coral on the eighteen-foot storm surge.

Driven by the hundred-and-twenty-mile-an-hour wind and a crushing eight-knot tidal current, the massive, burning wreckage of the cruise ship was drifting rapidly down the channel, surrounded by an enormous, undulating field of floating debris: wooden deck chairs, fiberglass lifeboats, cargo pallets, and thousands of dark, thrashing shapes riding the crests of the giant waves, heading straight for the walls of Fort Jefferson.

"Marcus!" Arthur screamed into his tactical radio. "They're in the surf! The cruise ship broke apart! The swarm is riding the storm surge!"
